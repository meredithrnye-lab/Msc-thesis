# Design and Manufacturing Feasibility Assessment of a Hybrid Jamming Soft Robotic Finger Exoskeleton for Hand Rehabilitation
**This repository contains dissertation work completed in partial fulfillment of the requirements for the degree MSc Rehabilitation Engineering and Assistive Technologies of University College London**

Authored by [Meredith Nye](https://www.linkedin.com/in/meredith-nye-89455a1b5/), with advisors [Rui Loureiro](https://www.linkedin.com/in/rui-cv-loureiro/) and [Peter Kyberd](https://www.linkedin.com/in/peter-kyberd-b78b1757/)

## Abstract
#### Background and Aims 
Hand impairment 
following stroke, arthritis, and age-related conditions requires high-repetition rehabilitation
that soft robotic exoskeletons are well suited
to deliver. Jamming-based variable-stiffness
mechanisms are promising but underdeveloped, typically offering only single-family, bi-
nary stiffness control. This project aimed to
evaluate the design and manufacturing feasibility of a hybrid granular–laminar jamming
soft robotic finger exoskeleton, hypothesising
that hybrid jamming could be integrated into
a compact finger architecture without compromising manufacturability.

#### Methods 
Sixteen design specifications were synthesised from hand exoskeleton literature.
A finger-level prototype was partially built
comprising a fabric pneumatic actuator over
a silicone hybrid jamming module (adapted
from an existing H-block design), with custom PCB control. Actuators were manually
inflated; the jamming module was cast across
four silicone iterations. Each specification was
rated on design and manufacturing axes and
combined into an overall feasibility rating.

#### Results 
The jamming geometry was rated Feasible on the design axis, but all
four casting iterations showed persistent tackiness and incomplete curing at fine layer-pocket
features, so no complete module
was assembled. Fabric actuators inflated with
one demonstrating bending but did not reach
target flexion pressure. No inferential statistics
were applied; findings rest on single-prototype
demonstration and inspection evidence.

#### Summary and Conclusions 
The hybrid jamming specification was rated Not Feasible
overall, only partially supporting the hypothesis. Fine mold geometry and fabrication constraints, not conceptual incompatibility, prevented reliable manufacture. Hybrid jamming
remains a credible route to graduated-stiffness
rehabilitation exoskeletons, but reliable small scale silicone manufacturing must be resolved
before clinical translation.
